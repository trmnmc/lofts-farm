Design experiment · Lofts farming & economy

Three ways to run your farm

You said the farming side is boring, and then you said why: it's watching paint dry. Those are two different diagnoses, so this tests both — before a developer spends a month on the wrong one.

Round 1 · today

The build you have

Six crops with the exact seed costs, sale values and grow times from your config.yml. Fixed prices. Harvests auto-collect and the Auto Merchant sells them. Turn-based, eight days.

Round 2 · decisions

Prices that move

Same crops, same costs. Prices shift overnight, harvests go to storage, you choose when to sell, and contracts become a hedge. Still turn-based, eight days.

Round 3 · your design

Slow crops, fast machines

Real time, three minutes. Crops stay slow — a pitcher runs half the round. The mill runs as fast as you can feed it, and it can always eat more than you can grow. Fertiliser halves a crop's remaining time, for a price. That gap is the whole design.

Round 3 measures two things. Dead air — seconds you had nothing to do — tests whether fast machines cover for slow crops. Mill starvation — seconds the machine sat with nothing to run — measures the supply gap, and whether it pushed you to reach for fertiliser or just annoyed you. It records the exact second you first did.
About eight minutes for all three.
